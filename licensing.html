Why Exclusive Licensing

Understanding why exclusivity is essential to preserving scarcity, premium value, and long-term brand equity in IP operations.

01

Multiple Licenses Severely Dilute IP Value

Granting licenses to multiple parties results in uneven product quality and frequent price wars, weakening the brand’s premium positioning.

Case Study: Hello Kitty

Hello Kitty adopted a broad multi-party licensing model in China, producing homogeneous stationery, snacks, appliances, and apparel. Excessive fragmentation caused rampant piracy and inconsistent quality, eventually reducing the IP to a “cheap sticker” in consumers’ minds.

02

Low Licensing Thresholds Undermine IP Scarcity

Low-barrier collaborations and overexposure mix high- and low-quality products, creating a perception of “no scarcity,” leading to aesthetic fatigue.

Case Study: Detective Conan

In November 2023, Naixue’s Tea, Lele Tea, DQ, and Paiyuefang all launched Conan-themed drinks. Initial hype faded immediately as oversupply saturated the market with low-quality products, including 19 RMB toothbrushes and 3–4 RMB collectible cards.

03

Exclusive Licensing Helps Standardize Products and Combat Piracy

Exclusivity ensures unified standards across pricing, quality, and channels—essential in protecting IP scarcity and combating piracy.

Case Study: Chiikawa

Official dolls cost 80–150 RMB; pirated versions only 30–50 RMB. Counterfeit circulation damages the IP’s premium perception. Miniso’s 2024 pop-up even saw misprinted dolls go viral—overshadowing official merchandise and blurring the line between genuine and pirated goods.

04

Exclusive Licensing Ensures Long-Term IP Management

Exclusivity allows control of collaboration frequency and categories, preventing overexposure and motivating long-term investment.

Case Study: Minions

The film launch brought a wave of Minions co-branded goods across FMCG and retail categories. Hype quickly faded afterward, and excessive collaborations resulted in consumer fatigue, reducing perceived value.

05

Conclusion

Exclusive licensing ensures centralized resource management, preserves scarcity, and maintains high-end positioning. By controlling collaboration frequency and categories, IP owners can retain fan interest and support sustainable long-term growth in Greater China and Southeast Asia.
